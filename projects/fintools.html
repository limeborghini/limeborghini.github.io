JPL Finance Tools Development

Skills: React, MySQL, OracleERP, PowerBI

Overview

While supporting the finance team within the Production Control department, I contributed to two process improvement initiatives:

  1. Cost Estimation Tool: Designed to help manufacturing teams quickly estimate costs for new builds and avoid overspending in weekly billing.

  2. Automated Billing Uploads: Maintained and improved a script to streamline billing data transfers to the Oracle ERP system, ensuring accuracy and proper filing.

Research

My team spoke with finance team members to understand how cost estimates were being calculated manually (a lot of math was done in Excel while figuring it out). We also had to learn about automating file uploads, OracleERP and its database, and how to send automated emails to LDAP groups for confirmation or errors.

Goals

  • Reduce time and effort required for teams to obtain accurate build cost estimates.
  • Eliminate errors and delays in weekly billing uploads.
  • Ensuring builds stayed within budget.

Obstacles

  • Many different coatings and components that made cost prediction challenging.
  • Testing with a non-Oracle database before connecting to the real one.
  • Updated security no longer allowed for access to the SharePoint we passed the files through.

Solution

  • Built a cost estimation tool that calculated build costs based on component selections, quantities, and custom additions.
  • Maintained the billing upload automation script, including error handling and automatic archival of submitted files.
  • Automated emails to the finance team with the returned files that needed review.

Results

  • Reduced cost estimation time from hours to minutes for manufacturing teams.
  • Provided finance with more accurate pre-billing data, lowering overspending incidents.
  • Automated billing uploads improved data accuracy and ensured timely submission to Oracle ERP.

Reflection

Working on these tools strengthened my ability to bridge technical solutions with finance and manufacturing needs. I also had to get comfortable working through the errors without assistance to check from the database submissions (Oracle lead please respond). Even though I'm not part of the team anymore, I worry on the constraints of the proposed budget knowing how much the department has to spend on hardware.
