Water Usage Data Analysis, Human-Computer Interaction Research

Skills: D3.js, research, eye-tracking, datasets


Overview

Under the guidance of Dr. Ben Steichen, I conducted a research project focused on human-computer interaction, using water usage data provided by teams from California State University, San Bernardino (CSUSB). The goal was to present this data in a way that could inform policy makers and encourage water conservation initiatives.

Research

I analyzed the county's water usage dataset and developed interactive visualizations to make trends and patterns more accessible to viewers. Leveraging the lab's eye-tracking systems, I observed which charts drew the most attention and engagement from participants. These findings were then used to refine the dashboard's design and highlight the most impactful data points. Throughout the project, I explored methods for presenting complex data in a way that could resonate with non-technical audiences and influence decision-making.

Goals

  • Create interactive visualizations to present county water usage trends.
  • Identify which visual formats most effectively engage viewers.
  • Provide policymakers with clear, compelling data to support water conservation actions.

Obstacles

  • Need to balance visual complexity with clarity for non-technical audiences.
  • Working with a dataset collected and structured by an external team, requiring extra cleaning and validation.
  • Coordinating research timelines alongside academic coursework.

Solution

  • Built a dashboard displaying multiple data visualizations for user testing.
  • Conducted usability studies to measure engagement with different chart types.
  • Refined visual designs to emphasize the data most likely to resonate with policymakers.

Results

  • Produced a polished, interactive dashboard tailored for public policy communication.
  • Delivered findings on visual engagement patterns to guide future water conservation outreach materials.

Reflection

Though it's not my most impressive work, this project marked my first long-term assignment concurrent with classwork and introduced me to the software development lifecycle, including requirement gathering, sprints, and code reviews. It was also my first exposure to data analytics, combining data handling with dashboard creation, and it ultimately sparked my interest in exploring JavaScript frameworks for interactive data presentation.
